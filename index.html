What you are hearing

The legend, in words. A colour bar is not a legend for a listener.

Hear the scale plays that legend instead of printing it: the bottom of the range, the balance point, the top, each named as it sounds. Spectral tilt is harder to read by ear than pitch, and pretending otherwise would be the same mistake as a picture with no colour bar. Giving the ear its three reference points first is the honest fix, and the residual runs during the preview too, so those three tones are held to the same contract the record is.

Where the numbers come from

Every track here starts at a picture, not at a dataset. NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio publishes the frame and, beside it, the colour bar that frame was painted with: a mapping from value to colour that someone else defined and stated. Every pixel is converted to a perceptually uniform colour space and matched to its nearest point on that bar, which returns a value and a distance. The distance is the test. A pixel that lands on the bar is data; one that lands far from it is basemap, coastline, label or annotation, and falls silent rather than being read as a low number. The Show discarded pixels button above darkens exactly those, so you can see what was thrown away.

Running a published bar backwards is a measurement because the bar was published. It is also only ever as faithful as that bar, so each recovered track prints how accurately its own colour bar inverts its own colours. That is the floor beneath every value it can produce.

That is also why nothing here is read from a numeric product, however convenient one would be. The challenge asks for the EIC Content Database to be used as data, and the pictures are what it publishes. A track built from a dataset we fetched ourselves would be answering a different question.

How to tell the residual is real

A number that always reads near zero is indistinguishable from a number that is hardcoded, so there is a control that breaks it. Break the encoder offsets what the carrier sounds by a quarter of the region's range and leaves what it reports untouched. The audio and the claim then disagree on purpose, and the residual jumps by the whole offset within a step and stays there until it is repaired. That is the demonstration that the measurement is reading the audio rather than echoing the payload.

What this instrument refuses to sonify

Not every EIC visualization can be inverted, and the difference decided what shipped here. These were examined and left out:

  • Volumetric CO₂ and CH₄ (SVS 5196, 5115, 5116): the pages describe colour only qualitatively. No range, no units, no bar.
  • GEOS aerosols (SVS 5669): several species in one picture, so a colour says which aerosol, not how much. There is no single quantity to recover.
  • Change in night lights (SVS 5313): the legend reads lights out, no change, new lights. Those are categories, and a category has no midpoint to balance two tones around.

Any value read out of those would have been invented here rather than recovered, and a soundscape with a residual beside it would claim a measurement that does not exist. The catalogue loader enforces the rule that produced this list: a track may not describe itself as measured without naming the published bar it came from, and the page refuses to start if one does.

A still frame has no time axis, so it is played by sweeping it, north to south for a map and top to bottom for a frame whose geography was never published, sounding the area-weighted mean of each band. The line on the frame shows where the sound is coming from.

No pitch anywhere in this instrument encodes anything. The two carrier partials never move, and the pad plays ordinary notes on an ordinary keyboard. The chord progression runs on a musical clock, four bars each, regardless of what the record is doing. The key selector is there to prove it: change key mid-playback and the residual does not move. Only the balance between the two partials carries the measurement, and it is held at each step's value until the next one.

Hiss under the tone is a coverage proxy: it grows as the share of the region actually observed at that step falls. It comes from the coverage figure shown in the readout, not from a published uncertainty estimate, and it is labelled a proxy because an unlabelled one would not be honest. Silence means no observation at all, never a value near the baseline.

Without the screen

Every frame carries a written description, the catalogue refuses to load a frame track that has none, and both canvases are marked decorative because everything in them is also printed, spoken and sounded. Speak values in the header announces the step, its measured value and the live residual every few seconds while the record plays, so the whole instrument including its own error can be followed by ear. It is throttled on purpose: a live region updated at the step rate queues faster than any screen reader drains it.

What the musical layer adds that the data does not contain

  • The chords and the progression. Four bars each on a musical clock, in whichever key you pick. Nothing about which notes sound encodes anything.
  • The brightness sweep. The pad's lowpass follows the value, mirroring the carrier's tilt. It is a knob, not an observable, which is exactly why the contract is not defined on it.
  • Reverb size, which opens from a room to a hall as observed coverage falls. Presentation of a real property of the data, not a measurement of it.
  • Delay feedback, which grows with the smoothed step-to-step change over a 4-second window. A smoothed derivative is a different quantity from a derivative.
  • The voicing, detune, envelopes, overdrive, the 5/16 and 7/16 delay divisions and the noise bed, all chosen by ear.

The carrier does none of these things. It is two pure sines at fixed frequencies whose balance is set only by the contract, and the validator's tap sits on the pair, before the mixer, so raising the pad, the reverb or the master fader cannot move the residual. Because the measurement is a ratio, even the listener's volume knob cancels out of it.
